Brand Positioning

Where Ember & Origin sits in the market, what it stands for, and how it speaks.

For coffee enthusiasts who believe that what's in the cup is inseparable from who grew it, Ember & Origin is the only specialty roaster that makes the farmer the hero of every bag — delivering single-origin, small-batch coffee alongside the story of the hands, land, and craft behind each roast.

Brand Pillars

I
Human Provenance

Every bag carries a real name, a real farm, a real story. We don't sell origins — we introduce people.

II
Radical Transparency

Roast profiles, processing methods, and farmer economics — visible, not buried in a blog post.

III
Intentional Craft

Small-batch means we can say no to commodity. Every roast decision is deliberate, never scaled.

IV
Earned Trust

We build community through education and authenticity — not discounts and manufactured urgency.

Voice & Tone

✍️

Written Voice

Warm, curious, grounded. Knowledgeable without being precious. Writes like a friend who spent a season in Ethiopia learning from a cooperative — never a brand manager who studied a style guide.

📸

Visual Voice

Earthy textures, natural light, hands-in-soil photography. The story card as a recurring visual motif. Restrained palette: ochre, terracotta, linen. No stock photography. No pristine minimalism.

🚫

Never Sound Like

Blue Bottle's cold editorial silence, Counter Culture's promotion-first urgency, or Stumptown's retro-quirky irreverence. Ember & Origin is warm, specific, and human — always.

5 Acquisition Strategies

Organic channels none of the three competitors are actively using — each uniquely enabled by the story card.

1

Farmer-Tagged Product Pages — Make the Farmer the Hero SKU

On every product listing, replace generic "origin notes" with a named farmer profile: photo, cooperative name, GPS coordinates, and a quote. This transforms a commodity SKU into a relationship. No competitor does this at the product level — Blue Bottle and Stumptown mention origins; none name the farmer on the product page itself.

Execution: Add a "Meet [Farmer Name]" section below Add to Cart. Link to a full farmer profile page. Include a downloadable PDF version of the story card for gift buyers who want to print and include it.

Why it converts: Specificity creates trust. A named farmer removes the abstraction that makes premium pricing feel arbitrary.
2

"Read Your Story Card" UGC Loop on Instagram

Create a branded hashtag (#EmberStoryCard or #MyOriginStory) and actively seed it. Ask every customer to photograph their story card and share it. Repost everything. The story card is a built-in physical marketing asset — no competitor has an equivalent artifact that customers naturally want to photograph and share on social.

Execution: Include a packing insert in every shipment with the hashtag and a one-line prompt ("Share your story."). Feature the best UGC weekly in Stories and monthly in a feed carousel. Offer 10% off next order for the best photo — targeted reward, not broad discounting.

Why it works: UGC is the highest-trust acquisition channel for DTC. The story card gives customers something worth photographing — a physical object with meaning, not just another bag.
3

Corporate Gifting via LinkedIn Outbound

Target HR leads, People Ops, and Office Managers at values-aligned companies (B Corps, tech, creative agencies) with a direct LinkedIn message: "We roast single-origin coffee and include a story card about the farmer with every bag. A lot of companies use us for employee gifting — happy to send a sample box." This is a zero-cost channel none of the three competitors actively use on LinkedIn.

Execution: Identify 20 target companies per week. Send 10–15 personalised messages/day (within LinkedIn free limits). Create a simple "Corporate Gifting" landing page with a Typeform inquiry form. Offer a free sample kit to serious prospects — cost of goods vs. B2B LTV is extremely favourable.

Why it converts: The story card is a uniquely gift-friendly product. Corporate buyers need something with a story to tell internally — Ember & Origin provides that narrative out of the box, literally.
4

Specialty Coffee Community Seeding (Reddit, Discord, Facebook Groups)

Blue Bottle, Stumptown, and Counter Culture are largely absent from specialty coffee communities (r/Coffee, r/espresso, specialty coffee Discord servers, Facebook groups like "Home Baristas"). Ember & Origin's story card concept is natively interesting to this audience — it aligns perfectly with their obsession over provenance and process.

Execution: Join 5–10 communities as a genuine participant, not a brand. Share sourcing stories, respond to origin questions, post educational content. Mention Ember & Origin organically — never pitch directly. Offer AMAs on sourcing or roasting. Budget 6–8 weeks before community trust yields meaningful referrals.

Why it converts: This is exactly where your customer lives. Trust built in a specialist community converts at dramatically higher rates than cold social ads.
5

Micro-Influencer Story Card Seeding (No Paid Sponsorships)

Identify 20–30 micro-influencers (3K–50K followers) in specialty coffee, sustainable food, or the conscious consumer space. Send them a free bag with a story card — no contract, no ask. The story card is novel enough that many will post organically. Stumptown and Counter Culture do influencer marketing; none do it around a physical artifact that tells a human story.

Execution: Use Instagram search + Modash free trial to identify accounts. DM with a personal note: "We think you'd appreciate what's on this story card." Ship the bag. Follow up once only. Track posts with brand mention alerts (Google Alerts, free tier). Build a private list of those who post for future collaboration.

Why it works: The story card does the selling. A genuine post from a coffee enthusiast with 8K followers outperforms a paid post from a 500K account in this category every time.

Section 05

3 Quick Wins for This Week

Concrete actions, sequenced by impact, achievable before Friday.

01

Publish Your First "Meet the Farmer" Instagram Post

The story card series is your core differentiator — get it into the feed this week. One post. One farmer. One real name. This is the seed the entire content strategy grows from, and it costs nothing but time.

  • Choose the farmer from your most recent or most interesting origin
  • Photograph the story card flat-lay with the bag (natural light, simple linen background)
  • Write a 150-word caption introducing the farmer by first name with one personal detail
  • Add 5–8 hashtags: #SpecialtyCoffee #SingleOrigin #CoffeeStory + 2 niche origin-specific ones
  • Post and respond to every comment within the first hour — algorithm priority window
⏱ 2–3 hours total
02

Launch the #EmberStoryCard UGC Hashtag

Your story card is a physical marketing asset that customers will photograph if you simply ask them. Start collecting UGC now — it will be your most powerful social proof within 30 days and requires zero budget.

  • Choose and verify your hashtag (search Instagram first to check for conflicts)
  • Add the hashtag + one-line prompt to your packing insert (create in Canva free if needed)
  • Post a Story asking your existing followers to reshare their story card photos
  • Email your last 20 customers directly with a personal ask to post and tag you
  • Pin the best response to your Instagram profile as a permanent Story Highlight
⏱ 1–2 hours setup · ongoing
03

Write and Publish a Founder LinkedIn Post

LinkedIn organic reach for personal posts is at a multi-year high. A founder story — why you put farmer names on bags — will outperform any company page post and plant the seed for B2B gifting inbound. Write it this week while the research is fresh.

  • Write 250–350 words, first person: what you saw, what changed, what you built
  • Do not use bullet points — LinkedIn's algorithm rewards paragraph prose for founder stories
  • End with a soft question: "Do you know the name of the person who grew your last cup?"
  • Post Tuesday or Wednesday 8–10am local time — peak B2B LinkedIn attention window
  • Reply to every comment within the first 2 hours — this is what drives further algorithmic distribution
⏱ 1 hour to write · 30 min to engage post-publish

Tracking Approach

Instagram Insights
Check weekly, not daily. Track reach, saves, and profile visits — not just likes. Saves signal content worth bookmarking; profile visits signal purchase intent.
LinkedIn Analytics
Focus on impressions and profile clicks from founder posts. A spike in profile clicks after a post = B2B curiosity. Follow up with those viewers proactively.
UTM Parameters
Tag all link-in-bio traffic with UTM params to separate Instagram and LinkedIn referrals in Google Analytics or Shopify. Critical for attributing first purchases correctly.
UGC Log
Maintain a spreadsheet of every story card post shared. Screenshot and save before they disappear. This archive becomes your social proof bank for future campaigns and website testimonials.
B2B Pipeline
Track inquiries in Notion or a spreadsheet. Note the source for each: LinkedIn post, outbound DM, community referral, or organic inbound. Source data informs where to double down in month two.
