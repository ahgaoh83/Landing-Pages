Section 02 · Marketing Action Plan

Nobody in specialty coffee names the farmer.

A gap analysis of the three dominant specialty coffee DTC brands — Blue Bottle, Stumptown, and Counter Culture — across eight dimensions of positioning, commerce, and community. The conclusion is narrower and more useful than "differentiate": one specific piece of territory is sitting unclaimed.

The Finding

Sustainability is claimed broadly. It is never personalized.

Every competitor talks about sourcing. None of them tell you who did the sourcing from, by name, on the thing you actually hold.

Core finding

None of the three competitors — Blue Bottle, Stumptown, or Counter Culture — lead with farmer identity or human provenance at the product level. Sustainability is claimed broadly but never personalized. The story card is Ember & Origin's uncontested territory.

Brand by Brand

Three strong operators, one shared blind spot

Each of these brands is genuinely good at something Ember & Origin is not yet good at. Read each profile as both a threat and a borrowable tactic.

Blue Bottle

The cold editorial
Where they're strong

Subscription is the central call to action, backed by a membership loyalty program. They almost never discount, which protects a genuinely premium price position.

Where they're exposed

No farmer storytelling, no human identity anywhere in the product experience, minimal social proof, and no discovery quiz. Education is parked in separate guides rather than sitting where people are shopping.

What we take

Their discipline on discounting. Their subscription-first architecture — without the editorial coldness.

Stumptown

The retro-quirky irreverent
Where they're strong

A multi-category subscription and a working Coffee Quiz for product discovery. Education is embedded as long-form content in the shopping experience rather than bolted on.

Where they're exposed

No farmer storytelling and no human identity at all. Social proof is only moderate, and there's no visible loyalty program — so repeat purchase rests entirely on brand affection.

What we take

The quiz mechanic and embedded education — repointed at origin and farmer rather than at flavor preference alone.

Counter Culture

The promotion-first urgency
Where they're strong

The most complete commerce stack of the three: a Coffee Quiz, an Earn Points loyalty program, embedded education, and social proof promoted all the way to the homepage hero.

Where they're exposed

Farmer storytelling exists only on origin pages, never at the product level, and human identity is absent. Prominent promotions steadily erode the premium they're asking customers to pay.

What we take

Social proof promoted to the homepage — sourced from #EmberStoryCard UGC rather than from review-count badges.

The White Space

What's actually unclaimed

Three dimensions where the competitive set scores zero and Ember & Origin can score highest — all three enabled by the same physical object.

I

Farmer storytelling on the product itself

Counter Culture manages it on origin pages; Blue Bottle and Stumptown not at all. Nobody puts a name on the bag. The story card makes this structural rather than a campaign.

II

Human identity as the brand's organizing idea

All three score zero. This isn't a feature they've deprioritized — it's a category-wide blind spot, which means it can be owned rather than merely competed on.

III

Education that ships in the box

Competitors treat education as web content. The story card puts it in the customer's hands at the moment of use — and makes it photographable, which web content never is.

The strategic read: don't try to out-commerce Counter Culture or out-restrain Blue Bottle. Win the dimension all three left empty — and let the story card carry the quiz, the social proof, and the subscription pitch along with it.

What To Do About It

The gaps, turned into a sequenced backlog

Every "opportunity" and "build one" cell in the matrix, ordered by how much it compounds the story card advantage rather than by how hard it is.

01
Farmer-tagged product pagesFarmer storytelling
Replace generic origin notes with a named farmer profile below Add to Cart — photo, cooperative, coordinates, quote. This is the one dimension no competitor occupies at product level.
Do now
02
#EmberStoryCard UGC loopSocial proof
Counter Culture proves homepage social proof works. Ours should be customer photographs of story cards, not review counts — a form of proof the others structurally cannot produce.
Do now
03
Subscription as the primary CTASubscription focus
Blue Bottle and Stumptown both lead with it; we currently don't. Frame it as a relationship rather than a replenishment: a new farmer, a new region, a new story each month.
Next
04
Origin discovery quizProduct discovery
Two of three competitors run one and it's table stakes for DTC coffee. Differentiate by matching people to a farmer and a place, not just to a roast level.
Next
05
Loyalty programRetention
Blue Bottle and Counter Culture both have one. Defer until subscription volume justifies it — and design it around collecting story cards rather than accumulating points.
Later
06
Hold the line on discountingPrice integrity
Not a build — a constraint. Counter Culture's prominent promotions are the cautionary case. Targeted rewards for UGC, never broad discounts that quietly come out of a farmer's price.
